# Weekly blog workflow

Open this website project in Codex and paste the prompt below. A new conversation is fine because the durable instructions, article sources and approval records live in the repo. You do not need to run shell commands yourself.

## Copy this prompt

Run this week's blog workflow for The Revenue Inbox. Prepare two substantial, useful articles for US B2B founders and sales leaders.

First read AGENTS.md, PRODUCT.md, DESIGN.md, EDITORIAL.md, SKILLS.md, the current SEO plan, existing articles and content briefs. Preserve the approved website, logo, typography, article layout, topic-specific cover style and contextual CTAs. Use the installed SEO, content strategy, research and copy-editing guidance, accounting for its documented runtime limitations.

Research current searches and competing results before choosing topics. Use Search Console and the connected keyword tools if available. If not, use public research and mark volumes, difficulty and rank positions as unmeasured. Do not invent demand or say we reviewed results we could not inspect. Prioritize useful questions with business fit and avoid competing with our existing URLs for the same intent.

Build a varied shortlist of five topics across templates, checklists, strategies, tutorials, tool comparisons, campaign teardowns and measurement. Briefly explain the intended reader, query, search intent, proposed contribution, evidence and inputs needed. Choose the strongest two that have enough evidence and continue drafting without waiting for a routine topic decision. Ask me only for specific missing facts; work on the other article while waiting. Do not fabricate operator experience or case results to fill gaps.

Write complete, substantial articles that solve the reader's problem before making an offer. For a broad guide, aim for the depth of roughly 1,500 to 2,500 useful words when the query warrants it. Use a shorter format when it answers the question fully. Never pad an article to meet a word count. Include usable examples, copyable templates, calculations, a worksheet or a clear process where appropriate. Explain limitations and failure cases. Distinguish original examples, proposed methods, assumptions and measured campaign evidence.

Use current primary sources for technical claims and product details, with citations beside the relevant statements. Use our approved case studies accurately, preserving units, timeframes and definitions. Name industry tools or experts only when they are relevant and properly attributed. Do not invent quotations, endorsements, partnerships, tests or statistics. No unsupported rankings, fake benchmarks, keyword stuffing, hype, em dashes, exclamation marks or generic AI intros.

Create accurate, enticing titles and search metadata. Give each article a relevant original cover and any visuals needed to explain the topic, using our established purple and neutral palette. Keep the answer and resources ungated. Place one well-matched strategy-call CTA after useful content and link naturally to related guides and relevant case evidence. Update older articles with useful links, preserving their approval requirements when source changes.

Save briefs, sources and review drafts in the established content folders. Build a protected noindex preview. Verify facts, links, metadata, image paths, responsive layout, accessibility, copy controls and calculators as applicable. Run the project's publication checks. Push the feature branch to GitHub and give me the preview, a short account of what each article adds and any facts I need to confirm.

Do not publish new articles until I approve their exact versions. Do not send outreach, buy links or post to external platforms. Flag a genuinely relevant backlink or distribution opportunity and prepare a draft only. Do not claim automatic weekly execution or guaranteed Google rankings.

## After reviewing the preview

Reply: "I approve [article titles or slugs]. Publish those versions live through alive, update sitemap/RSS and internal links, and verify the live pages."

## Working rhythm

Begin with two reviewed articles weekly. Add a short voice note, redacted email, screenshot or campaign lesson whenever available; this is the most useful input for original content. Revisit performance after 30 and 90 days using actual Search Console and booking data. Refresh existing pages where the evidence favors that over another new article. No scheduled writer or publisher is running by default.
